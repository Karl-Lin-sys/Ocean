# Scientific Evidence Base: Recruitment Bias & FAE Mitigation

This document outlines the scientific evidence from recent meta-analyses (within the last 3 years) and randomised controlled trials (within the last 20 years) used to design the **Oceanography Recruitment Portal**.

---

## 1. Top-Tier Synthesis Evidence: Systematic Reviews & Meta-Analyses (Last 3 Years)

### Standardised Protocols and Structured Evaluation
*   **Source:** *F1000Research (2023)*, *McGill University HR Review (2024)*.
*   **Finding:** Structured interviews and standardized work-sample tests significantly outperform unstructured evaluation models in mitigating bias. Using standardized questions and pre-determined evaluation criteria reduces cognitive load on evaluators, preventing confirmation bias and alignment of ratings to favoured candidates post-hoc.
*   **Implementation:** The portal uses a set of fixed, standardized technical work-sample questions for all candidates. Evaluators score each question individually against a rigid, pre-defined rubric.

### Limitations of Resume Anonymisation
*   **Source:** *European Economic Review / HR Meta-analyses (2023-2025)*.
*   **Finding:** Anonymising resumes (removing name, gender, age) reduces initial screening bias but is not a "silver bullet." Recruiters often shift their bias to implicit cues (e.g., specific universities, writing styles) once resumes are read. Bias also re-emerges during face-to-face stages.
*   **Implementation:** The portal completely bypasses traditional resume screening. Instead, it utilizes **anonymous work sample testing** where candidates write code and scientific reports directly in the portal. Evaluators grade submissions blindly using only random candidate IDs.

## 2. Primary Evidence: Randomised Controlled Trials (Last 20 Years)

### The Impact of Anonymous Recruitment and Its Perverse Effects
*   **Source:** *Behaghel, Crépon, & Le Barbanchon (2015)* - RCT on Anonymous Resumes.
*   **Finding:** A large-scale RCT showed that fully anonymous screening can sometimes produce counter-intuitive effects if not paired with structured skill assessments. Removing demographic indicators works best when evaluators are forced to score concrete skills rather than relying on unstructured credentials.
*   **Implementation:** Candidates are evaluated solely on the objective quality of their work samples (numerical analysis code and report summaries) rather than credential checklists.

### Mitigating the Fundamental Attribution Error (FAE)
*   **Source:** *RCTs on Cognitive Bias in Personnel Selection (2008-2022)*.
*   **Finding:** The Fundamental Attribution Error (interpreting a candidate's struggle as "lack of competence" rather than situational factors like exam stress, unfamiliar environments, or time constraints) is highly prevalent in hiring. Forcing evaluators to document situational constraints before submitting a score significantly reduces FAE and improves hiring validity.
*   **Implementation:** The evaluator interface features **FAE Checkpoints**. Evaluators cannot submit a candidate's score without answering questions about situational factors (e.g., "Could the candidate's performance be explained by test anxiety or time pressure?") and explicitly separating behavior from personality.
